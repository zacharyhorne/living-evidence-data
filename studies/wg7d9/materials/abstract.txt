We investigated folk intuitions about personal identity—what makes someone the same person over time. Although psychological continuity (ie, continuity in mental states) is typically seen as an important criterion for personal identity, it is not sufficient: Many also believe that one must have the same body, or be composed of the same matter, to be the same person (physical continuity). This belief, however, may be scientifically questionable. Why, then, do people endorse it? In four studies with adults and children, we show that physical-continuity intuitions are compelling because they emerge from primitive essentialist biases.
